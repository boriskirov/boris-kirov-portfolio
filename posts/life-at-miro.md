[Miro](https://www.miro.com) is an online collaborative whiteboard platform, a canvas-based product that focuses on collaboration and teamwork. As of today, Miro is an established leader in visual collaboration. My work there stretches from all aspects of design, from product definition, strategy alignment, feature releases, and launches to experimentation and research.

### History

I joined Miro pretty early, as the first full-time Platform designer. This was before the pandemic of Covid-19. Things back then were quite different for Miro, it was a small-sized startup with about 250 employees and a newly opened office in Amsterdam. I was also the first designer hired for the Amsterdam office. Today 2022, Miro is one of the most successfully growing SaaS companies in the world with more than 1200 Mironeers across multiple locations in the world.

### Platform team

Miro Platform is a unique sub-service that allows for external or internal parties to generate solutions on top of Miro. They can build applications, which can serve as applications or integrations by consuming the REST API or the Web SDK of Miro. My participation involved multiple teams and initiatives in the Platform.

![Life at Miro](../../life-at-miro-1.png)

As a single Platform designer, in the beginning, I became the designer to go to if anything is needed or related to Platform, I was a Lead Designer for the stream. This allowed me to observe and create a holistic vision of how the Platform can evolve and direct from an experience point of view both for end-users and for app creators. You can break it down into developer onboarding, app creation, developer experience to end-user app discovery, and core mechanics and logic of objects on the canvas.

![Life at Miro](../../platform-adoption-framework.png)

### Panel importance

One of the biggest and most important projects that I worked on was to collaborate and contribute to the panels UX in Miro. We were looking for a scalable way to empower external creators to inject external applications in Miro and to provide a native behavior to end-users. Solving unlimited scenarios from gallery-focused applications supported by Drag’n’Drop API to automation and configuration of widget or application behavior.
The design was inspured by OS, where similar panels allow for control and flexible information structure. We also thought about allowing developers to configure behavior, like scale and app experience.

![DCI P3 range](../../panel-ux-1.png)

**Lesson learned**

While working on the Panel UX, we identified as a team the need to provide well-structured layouts. That way our creators wouldn't spend time dealing with composition issues, they can quickly inject data and start prototyping and testing their solutions. That’s how the [App Layouts](https://www.mirotone.xyz/docs#gallery-template) in Mirotone was born. You will learn more about Mirotone, a bit later in this story.

### Installation and Authorization

Installation and Authentication is one of the most important parts of the Platform journey, it’s the part where end-users are deciding to start using an application for their daily work in Miro.

We’ve worked on a full redesign of this flow and created a new conversation with dynamic and scalable UI. The UI allowed for different combinations of scopes based on the application configuration and single click on installation. We’ve conducted multiple user validations and interviews to come up with the optimal solution inspired both by single users to big enterprise teams.

![Installation-and-auth](../../i-a-1.png)

![Installation-and-auth](../../i&a-blocks.gif)

Once we introduced installation and authorization, we’ve started focusing on Enterprise experience and Admin management. As a leading designer, I focused on the strategic vision and the direction in which we wanted to take this initiative as a product area.

![Installation-and-auth](../../i-a-2.png)

**Lesson learned**

The most important lesson from the focused work on installation and authorization was the value of conversation and clear messaging. Scopes and Authorization remains a technical area that's still considered confusing for users. Especially in the context of data and privacy. Based on our research we focused kept the focus on the conversation between the UI and the user, through dynamic and reusable data blocks which allowed for different scenarios.

### Async collaboration for the win

After we introduced the new authentication mechanism for our app users, leading the experience for the Platform meant to oversee the areas which can be impacted and should correlate to this massive improvement. Which was the app config page.

As we lacked engineering capacity for such a redesign, I stepped in and directly worked in the codebase to provide a simple but effective page re-design to allow for our Platform to scale in multiple directions. What is amazing in Miro is that collaboration is a mindset. We used our canvas to involve product, engineering, design, and Platform leadership for an asynchronous critique, which resulted in an aligned vision of how we want to communicate things to our app creators.

![Async collaboration](../../async.png)

**Lesson learned**

Asynchronous critiques are scalable, effective, and powerful when prepared in a good way. Writing makes the product knowledge durable, it requires time for preparation and helps the structure of the thought.

### Mirotone

![Mirotone](../../mirotone-reveal2.gif)

Teaming up with some of my fellow Developer experience engineers, we created [Mirotone](https://www.mirotone.xyz). Mirotone is a small & lightweight design system kit that tries to match Miro styles for helping you design, build and integrate apps. The kit was designed with a focus on internal control and external flexibility.

![Mirotone](../../mirotone-1.png)

Mirotone has +9K downloads on NPM today and keeps growing daily, a dedicated team, and an additional [Figma plugin](https://www.figma.com/community/plugin/1040629471915005702/Mirotone-icons) to try to close the funnel and to allow for a delightful experience while building.

![Mirotone](../../mirotone-2.png)
